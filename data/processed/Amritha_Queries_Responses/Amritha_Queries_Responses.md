## Continental (Terrestrial) Environments

## Fluvial (Rivers)

- e Straight channels: Minimal sinuosity, high-energy, coarse sediment.
- e Meandering channels: High sinuosity, point bars, oxbow lakes, fine sediment.
- e Braided channels: Multiple shifting channels, bars, high sediment load.
- e Anabranching/anastomosing: Multiple semi-stable channels separated by vegetated islands.

## Alluvial Fans

- e Proximal fan: Closest to source, coarse gravel, debris flows.
- e Mid-fan: Mix of sand and gravel, transitional deposits.
- e Distal fan: Finer sediments, often reworked by water/wind, higher sorting.

## Aeolian (Desert Dunes)

- e Dune fields: Active or stabilized dunes, various shapes (barchan, transverse, parabolic).
- e Interdune areas: Flat zones between dunes, may contain ephemeral lakes.
- e Sand sheets/loess: Extensive, flat deposits of wind-blown silt or sand.

## Lacustrine (Lakes)

- e Lake margins: Sandy to muddy, influenced by wave activity.
- e Deep lake/central basin: Fine-grained sediments, low energy.
- e Deltaic/fan deltas: Where rivers or fans enter the lake, may form Gilbert-type deltas.

## Transitional Environments

## Deltaic

- e River-dominated: Channel mouth bars, distributary channels, levees.
- e Wave-dominated: Smooth shorelines, beach ridges, low distributary numbers.
- e Tide-dominated: Sand ridges, tidal flats, dendritic channels.

## Estuarine &amp; Tidal Flats

- e Channels: Subtidal, tidal currents, mixed energy.
- e Intertidal flats: Mud/sand flats, exposed at low tide, high organic activity.
- e Supratidal marshes: Above high tide, vegetated, organic-rich.

## Barrier Island &amp; Lagoonal Systems

- e Barrier core: Shoreface, foreshore, backshore.
- e Tidalinlets: Openings allowing tidal exchange.
- e Back-barrier/lagoon: Protected, low energy, fine sediments and marshes.

## Marine Environments

## Shallow Marine (Shelf)

- e Inner shelf: Proximal, wave-action, sand and shell beds.
- e Middle shelf: Mixed sediment, less energy, faunal diversity.
- e Outer shelf: Muddy, low energy, bioturbation.

## Carbonate Platforms &amp; Reefs

- e Fore-reef: High-energy slope, broken coral debris.
- e Reef crest: Main reef, massive coral growth.
- e Back-reef/lagoon: Protected, fine sediments, sometimes stromatolites.

## Deep Marine (Turbidites &amp; Submarine Fans)

- e Submarine channeUlevee: Coarse deposits from turbidity flows.
- e Lobe/fan complexes: Finer turbidite sequences, graded bedding.
- e Basin plain: Pelagic muds, low-energy fine sediments.

All properties provided in LAS files can be plotted against depth or measured depth (MD), with the property values on the X-axis and depth increasing downward on the Y-axis (an inverted depth scale).

Among these, the gamma ray (GR) log is often the most relevant for identifying depositional environments because it effectively distinguishes between shale-rich and clean sand/shale intervals, helping to characterize lithology and sedimentary facies.
